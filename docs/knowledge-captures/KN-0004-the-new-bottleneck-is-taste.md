# Knowledge Capture Note: The New Bottleneck Isn’t Code—It’s Taste

- **Knowledge ID:** `KN-0004`
- **Cycle or commit:** First Workflow v2 editorial intake; uncommitted
- **Date:** `2026-07-27`
- **Trigger:** A practitioner built a functional application with an AI-assisted development platform, then sought help making the interface feel less AI-made.
- **Reusable insight:** As AI lowers the cost of constructing functional software, differentiation increasingly depends on judgment: hierarchy, restraint, constraints, interaction design, and accumulated taste.
- **Evidence:** The builder reported that the running/walking territory application was functional. The remaining concern was its AI-made appearance. Community feedback emphasized studying strong products and understanding layout, hierarchy, constraints, and interaction design; it treated color, font, and effects changes as insufficient by themselves.
- **Inference:** This case suggests that implementation capacity may be becoming easier to acquire than design judgment. The emerging bottleneck may therefore shift from producing working interfaces to making coherent editorial and design decisions.
- **Claim boundary:** One Reddit discussion does not establish an industry-wide trend or prove that taste is always the primary software bottleneck. The result may also reflect current AI defaults, templates, prompting, or platform conventions. It does not establish that human-built interfaces are automatically better. Additional cases and stronger sources are required before Canon promotion.

### Inferred

- AI-assisted construction may expose design judgment as a more visible differentiator.

- The case aligns with PasteLint’s preference for typography, hierarchy, restraint, a clear primary action, and keeping the transformation visually central.

## Proposed article direction

**Central question:** What happens when building the interface becomes easier than deciding what deserves emphasis?

**Thesis:** AI can accelerate software construction, but it does not automatically provide hierarchy, restraint, interaction judgment, or taste. As implementation becomes more accessible, those editorial decisions become more visible and more valuable.
