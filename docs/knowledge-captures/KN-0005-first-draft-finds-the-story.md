- **Working article title:** *The First Draft Finds the Story. Revision Makes It Work.*
- **Date:** `2026-07-27`
- **Trigger:** A request to study recognized film-industry practitioners discussing strong and weak scripts and connect their observations to the value and limits of revision.
- **Central question:** What can a writer know at the end of a first draft that was impossible to know at the beginning?
- **Reusable insight:** A first draft gives the writer new knowledge about the work's actual subject, structure, setup, emphasis, excess, collaborators, and constraints. Revision applies that later knowledge backward across the work.

## Bounded synthesis

The sources directly support that experienced screenwriters treat drafts as changeable and that revision may involve discovering what the story became, returning to earlier pages, reconsidering openings and setup, sharpening language, removing material, responding to collaborators and production circumstances, and allowing the story to evolve.

Together they support a bounded practitioner synthesis: **revision is retrospective alignment**. A writer reaches the end with knowledge unavailable at the beginning, then revises earlier choices using that knowledge. A later problem may expose an earlier setup failure, while collaboration and constraint may test assumptions that drafting alone did not test.

This synthesis belongs to PasteLint's editorial analysis. It is not a formal theory stated jointly by the speakers and should not be attributed directly to any one of them.

## Inference and product relevance

A revision system is most useful when it helps a writer locate misalignment without claiming to know the work's true meaning automatically. Second Draft can reveal exact eligible repetition, remove bounded filler, preserve uncertainty, modality, recommendation strength, actors, timing, and protected values, and expose changes for review. It should not substitute its own interpretation for what writing the draft taught the writer.

Working PasteLint takeaway: **A revision tool should help writers align the draft with what writing the draft taught them. It should not substitute its own interpretation for that discovery.**

## Claim boundaries
